Why this matters

Every routing and congestion-pricing model that uses the Bureau of Public Roads (BPR) function assumes an exponent β near 4. Calibrated over the near-recurrent peak period instead of a fixed clock window, the exponents found here are consistently near 1 — and conventional fixed-window units sometimes produce theoretically-inadmissible negative exponents, which never happens with the near-recurrent unit. See the underlying paper for the full derivation and statistical tests.
